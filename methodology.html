2 · Extraction Rules

Structure first.
No guessing.

The parser deliberately uses fixed text patterns and standard section headings rather than machine learning. That keeps every result explainable and repeatable.

  • Where it reads. The first six pages of each report. That window reliably covers the title page and the three standard OAG sections: the Report on the Financial Statements; the Report on Lawfulness and Effectiveness in Use of Public Resources; and the Report on Effectiveness of Internal Controls, Risk Management and Governance.
  • How it splits findings. Numbered headings (“1. Title”, “2. Title”) are the primary, high-confidence signal that one finding ends and the next begins.
  • When there's no clear signal. If a section has no numbered findings, the whole section is kept as one block and flagged for manual review. It is never split on a guess.
  • What it strips. Standard audit boilerplate, such as the ISSAI citation wording and pure clean-opinion text, is removed so it can't be counted as a finding.

Three parser updates got the extraction to this point:

UpdateWhat we fixedResult
v3.6A handful of report titles were unreadable because of a formatting quirk in a few PDFs.6 reports that had failed to process were recovered.
v3.7The tool didn't recognize every way audit reports word their standard legal disclaimers.6 rows that were just boilerplate text, not real findings, were removed.
v3.8Real findings were sometimes getting cut off by the standard disclaimer text sitting in front of them.534 rows fixed (11% of the dataset): 234 real findings were recovered in full, and 300 rows that weren't real findings were correctly removed.

The largest, v3.8, re-anchored extraction to the real “Basis for Qualified / Adverse / Disclaimer of Opinion” heading — the source of the 534-row fix above.

3 · S.I.N.S. Pillar Tagging

212 findings.
251 tags.

Each finding is checked against a curated keyword set for each pillar. Keywords must match as whole words, and the entity's own name is excluded from the search so an organisation called “…Network…” can't tag itself.

PillarLayerExample keywordsFindings tagged
SystemsFoundationIFMIS, ERP, application system, audit trail134
InfrastructureOperationalserver, data centre, backup, disaster recovery59
SecurityGovernanceIT security, access control, authentication36
NetworkConnectivitynetwork, LAN/WAN, firewall, connectivity22
Pillar tags in total251

A finding can carry more than one tag. An ERP-governance finding, for example, can be tagged both Security and Systems. So the pillar counts add up to more than the number of findings behind them:

  • 212 unique findings (4.8% of 4,406) carry at least one digital-pillar tag.
  • 33 of those carry more than one tag, adding 39 extra tags.
  • 212 + 39 = 251 pillar tags.
  • The remaining 4,194 findings (95.2%) match no pillar keyword and are grouped as “Other”: financial and governance findings. That is expected, since OAG audits are financial-statement audits, not IT audits.

Keyword tagging is a signal, not a validated classification. Treat these counts as a floor for how often digital risk appears in the audit record, not as a measured prevalence.

5 · Measuring Recurrence

Same problem.
Next audit.

RETACH's Cyber Risk Quantification (CRQ) Engine estimates Expected Annual Loss as EAL = Σ(frequency × severity). This dataset supplies frequency-side evidence only. Severity (what each type of finding costs) is a separate component, not yet built.

Measure 1: self-reported (19.8%). The share of the 4,406 findings where the auditor's own wording points to a prior-year issue, with phrases like “as previously reported” or “similar observation was made in the prior year.” It only counts when the auditor chose to cross-reference, so it undercounts.

Measure 2: content-matched (25.6%). Does the same underlying issue persist for the same entity across audit years, whether or not the auditor said so? The method:

  1. Within each entity, normalise every finding title by stripping numbers, amounts and generic audit boilerplate.
  2. Compare titles from different financial years. Link any pair with at least 50% word overlap (Jaccard similarity ≥ 0.5).
  3. Group linked findings into “issue threads”: chains of the same problem across years.
  4. Exclude titles dominated by currency figures. One mis-parsed reconciliation table initially produced five false “recurring findings” before this guard was added; they were removed after manual review.

Result: 458 issue threads across 243 entities, containing 1,126 findings (25.6%). 341 of those threads run in consecutive years with no clean year in between, which is the stronger signal.

Neither 19.8% nor 25.6% is a validated actuarial coefficient. Making either one a defensible EAL input still needs entity-level normalisation, time-decay weighting, and testing against a held-out sample. Only 18 of the 458 threads tag to a S.I.N.S. pillar; the named examples on the main page are a proof-of-concept slice, not a prevalence rate for digital-risk recurrence.
6 · Entity Coverage Tiers

To decide how deep entity-level profiles can go, each of the 562 entities is placed in a tier by how many digital-tagged findings it has in the register.

TierRuleEntities

B · Limited1–2 digital-tagged findings108
C · NoneNo digital-tagged findings (352 with other findings, 81 with no findings at all)433
Total562

A tier reflects how much keyword-tagged audit evidence exists, not how risky an entity is. Tier C does not mean “no digital risk”: it means the OAG record we hold doesn't surface it. Entities audited in fewer years have less chance to reach Tier A.

7 · Additional Limitations

Two more gaps
worth knowing.

The main page lists the network false-positive risk, the scanned-file gap, year-mismatch drift and truncation. See those here. Two more apply:

Entity-name spelling variants

11 confirmed cases of the same entity appearing under slightly different names across years (e.g. “Regional Centre on Groundwater Resource” vs. “…Resources”). Left unmerged on purpose: merging without manual confirmation risks combining two different entities.

Recurrence-thread precision

A 50% word-overlap threshold can occasionally link related but distinct findings that share generic phrasing (e.g. “Non-Performing Loans to Customers” vs. “…to Directors” for the same entity). Consecutive-year runs are also unreliable for the few entities whose financial year doesn't end in June, because their year labels don't line up in sequence.

Tier A source-verification precision

Digital-finding tags were checked by hand against the source OAG reports for the 21 highest-count entities: 79% of tagged findings (59 of 75) were confirmed; 14 further digital findings the tagger missed were found in the same reports. Most false positives were findings about the accounting treatment of ICT assets rather than ICT control failures. One row (Technical and Vocational Education and Training Authority, FY2022/23) could not be checked — its source report is a scanned image with no extractable text. Missed findings were searched only within reports already reviewed.

8 · QA & Validation

Tested before
it ships.

  • Every parser update (v3.6, v3.7, v3.8) was regression-tested against a 40-file, hand-validated baseline before running on the full corpus.
  • Automated checks cover: placeholder rows, scanned and year-mismatch rates against known baselines, pillar-distribution sanity, duplicate rows, garbled entity names, and per-file finding-count outliers.
  • After v3.8, a stratified manual sample of 171 rows (about 3.5–5% of the register) was reviewed line by line against the source reports. It found zero regressions and no recurrence of previously fixed defect types.
  • The 15 duplicate downloads were resolved with a documented reason for each (see section 1).
9 · How to Cite & Data Access

Using this data.

If you quote figures from the index, please cite it as:

RETACH Digital Ltd. (2026). Kenya Digital Risk Index: OAG Kenya Findings Register (Version 3.8) [Data set]. https://kdri.retach.ke/

Please quote the version number. Figures can change between register versions.

What's public. The aggregate statistics on this site and in register_public_v38_stats.json.

What isn't. The row-level register, the recurrence-thread data and the extraction pipeline are not published. The underlying OAG reports are public documents, available from the Office of the Auditor-General.

Access and partnerships. For research access or partnership enquiries, contact info@retach.tech.

10 · Disclaimer

This dataset is derived from publicly available OAG Kenya audit reports and is provided for research, benchmarking and risk-quantification purposes. It is not a legal, financial or audit opinion. RETACH Digital Ltd makes no representation about the completeness or accuracy of the underlying source documents beyond what this methodology discloses. Findings are extracted and tagged by software and validated through the QA process above; confirm any entity-level conclusion against the source report before relying on it for a decision.
